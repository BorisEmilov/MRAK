## 1. The Unclean Days — _Mrasni dni_ (MVP)

**Folklore [F].** The twelve days between Christmas and Theophany (Yordanovden) are "unbaptized":
the waters are unclean, and **karakondzhuli** and other spirits roam. People avoid going out after
dark, draw **tar crosses** on doors, keep the Christmas log (_badnik_) burning. On Christmas Eve
(_Budni vecher_) the table holds an odd number of meatless dishes. **Koledari** — young men singing
carols house to house from midnight — protect households for the year. On New Year (St Basil's day)
children bless people with a decorated cornel branch (_survachka_). At **Theophany** the priest
throws a cross into the water; the waters are blessed and the unclean ones flee.

**In game [I].**

- 12 nights; karakondzhuli active; night length +20%.
- Night 1: koledari walk the village — escort them to keep their protection (+Faith, −Hysteria).
- Night 7 (New Year): _survakane_ event; good-luck omen on success.
- Night 12: eve of Theophany — the final exorcism window for M1. At dawn the blessed water ends
  the chapter; karakondzhuli vanish.
- Theophany holy water is the strongest MVP consumable.

## 2. Kukeri

**Folklore [F].** Men in heavy masks, sheepskins and huge bells dance through the village to drive
away evil spirits and wake fertility, usually on Cheesefare Sunday before Lent (dates and names
vary: kukeri, survakari, babugeri). A ritual ploughing and sowing often follows.

**In game [I].** Wearing a kuker costume hides the guardian from demons for a night (Notice reset)
but villagers cannot recognise them either. The kukeri procession is a defensive set-piece: the
louder the bells, the more lesser besove are driven out of houses (hidden possessions surface).

## 3. Nestinari

**Folklore [F].** Fire-dancing on embers, with icons, on the feast of Sts Constantine and Helena —
a rite of the **Strandzha** region (south-east Bulgaria), not of the Rhodopes. The dancers
(_nestinari_) enter a trance led by the drum and bagpipe.

**In game [I].** Because the rite is not Rhodopean, MRAK justifies it explicitly: a Strandzha
nestinarka, Zlata, married into Mrak in 1830 and keeps the rite in secret. This transplantation is
**invented** and must be presented as unusual within the world. The chapter confronts **Ognitsa,
the Ember** (the demon who fed on Stana's pyre).

## 4. Rusalia Week — _Rusalska nedelya_

**Folklore [F].** The week after Pentecost when **samodivi** (also called _rusalii_) roam the
fields and waters; people avoid working, swimming or sleeping outside. In north-western Bulgaria
male bands (_rusaltsi_) performed healing dances for those "taken" by the samodivi.

**In game [I].** The forest spirit's power peaks. Samodivi dance circles spread to the village
edge. A healing-dance mini-rite can cure villagers "taken" by the forest.

## 5. Enyovden

**Folklore [F].** Midsummer (24 June, nativity of St John the Baptist). Herbs are gathered before
sunrise, when they have the most power; people roll in the dew and look at their shadow at sunrise
(a missing or partial shadow foretells illness). Witches and samodivi gather herbs the same night.

**In game [I].** Herbs gathered on Enyovden morning have double potency
([alchemy](../systems/alchemy.md)). **The Zlatevi renew the Pact on Enyovden night** at the
crossroads: the central set-piece of the "expose" and "steal" routes. Also the anniversary of Stana's burning.

## 6. Zadushnitsa

**Folklore [F].** All Souls' Saturdays (several per year; the autumn one precedes Archangel
Michael's day): families visit graves, pour wine, light candles and share food (wheat _kolivo_) for
the dead.

**In game [I].** The dead may speak to the guardian on Zadushnitsa night. M2 _The Empty Graves_
takes place here. Ancestors (previous guardians) can appear and give one journal entry back.
